1. Product summary

Oktavia SP is the Oktavia installation for service providers: the carrier service orchestrator that runs the core and the edge of the network —the core P routers and route reflectors, the PE at each point of presence and the customers' CE— and keeps, on the same platform, each customer's base of contracted services. It is not a separate product: it is the same Oktavia Pro loop —audit, compare with the intent, propose in the Digital Twin, approve, apply, verify and audit again— with a service catalog and screens of its own for providers.

It is lab-tested with Cisco IOS XR (XRv9000 24.3) in the core and PE, and Cisco IOS on the customer CE.

RoleSite typeDevicesAccess
P and route reflectorcoreCore, Cisco IOS XRNETCONF / YANG
PEpopProvider edge, Cisco IOS XRNETCONF / YANG
CEcustomerCustomer equipment, Cisco IOSSSH (CLI)

What sets Oktavia SP apart: a customer service is declared once and Oktavia builds the PE and the CE; the IOS XR core changes as a single transaction with confirmed commit; each contracted service has a row with its real network state and the fields the provider defines without coding; service profiles change all their services in a single change; and engineers create new services and parameters from the device's YANG models, without writing service packages. No change reaches the network without a person approving it.

2. The approach

Intent per service, not per device

Intent is declared per service, in the style of L3SM and L2SM: a customer service is declared once —the customer, its sites or circuits and the contracted bandwidth— and Oktavia distributes it to every PE and CE involved. Nobody edits device by device.

Each customer's numbering plan is derived from its identifier: the customer AS, the RTs, the RD per PE, the /31 PE-CE links, the pseudowire EVI and the subnets. Two customers never collide and the same customer is numbered the same way on every creation.

Model-driven on IOS XR

IOS XR devices are managed over NETCONF with YANG models: the Cisco-IOS-XR-um-* models for configuration and the native operational models for state. No screen scraping.

Closed loop with a network transaction

The core is not changed device by device: all the IOS XR devices in the change go into a single transaction. If one rejects the change or the verification fails, it is cancelled on all of them.

Deterministic first

Service creation and retirement, numbering, service state, customer impact and creating a service from a configuration are computed without AI, with reproducible results. AI —local, on the server, or Claude— is used to explain, draft and prioritize, and every action says before you click whether it uses it.

The provider tenant recognizes itself

When an organization has IOS XR devices or P, PE or RR roles, Oktavia treats it as a provider network: the domains become Core · Managed services · Security · Management (in an enterprise they are Campus · WAN · Security · Management). Core is the P, the route reflector and the core side of the PE (IS-IS, Segment Routing, TI-LFA, BFD and core BGP); Managed services is what runs from the PE to the CE, what each customer buys. The change shows in Argos, Architecture, Telemetry, the Main dashboard and the analyses; campus flows —VLANs, port-security, FlexVPN and the campus test network— are hidden, and Customer services and the NETCONF Dictionary appear. Other tenants do not change.

Viewing and testing an IOS XR service

IOS XR has no Jinja template: the NETCONF lane builds the YANG XML from the same values. On the service page, testing with a device shows its XML, with the device's values highlighted to tell them apart from what the lane fixes. See what changes and Test these values compare today's NETCONF plan with the one for the change, as the Digital Twin would push it, with confirmed commit. This applies to core and customer services. 5. Customer services

Customer services is the provider's base of contracted services, joined to the network state. Commercial and provisioning data live here; the customer's configuration stays in the intent and reaches the network through the Digital Twin.

One table: provisioning and network state

One row per contracted service —L3VPN, pseudowire or dedicated internet— with:

  • Base columns: customer, service ID, type, profile, real network state (up, partial, down or no data), site, PE ↔ CE and contracted bandwidth, plus the provider's custom fields.
  • Network and base, together: a service that exists in the network without a row in the base also appears, and each row says whether it is in the network, only in the base or retired.
  • Search, filters and paging: by type, customer, profile, state and any custom field; filter lists are capped with "and N more", designed for thousands of services.
  • Column chooser: the base columns and the custom fields each user wants to see; the choice is remembered.
  • CSV export with the filters applied.
  • Per-row actions: end-to-end state, edit data and fields, retire from the network with Argos, retire or reactivate in the base and delete from the base.
  • New service: L3VPN, pseudowire or dedicated internet with Argos —it saves the intent and proposes the change in the Twin— or only in the base, as a commercial record (it creates the customer if it does not exist).

Custom fields, defined without coding

Each provider defines the columns it provisions with —change number, data center, delivery VLAN, EVI, technical contact…— from the screen itself. Types: text, number, list, yes/no, IPv4 prefix, VLAN and inventory device. Each field applies to the customer or to the service, can be limited to a service type, is grouped and can be required. Values are validated on save, and renaming a field keeps its data. Fields appear in the table, its filters, the export and the Argos creation forms.

Service profiles

A profile is the product the provider sells per service type ("Gold L3VPN", "Silver dedicated internet"): its network parameters and the default values of the custom fields.

Service typeProfile network parameters
L3VPNContracted bandwidth and SR-TE color.
PseudowireContracted bandwidth and SR-TE color.
Dedicated internetMTU (576 to 9216).
  • A service created with a profile inherits it; values entered by hand take precedence over the profile.
  • When a profile that already has services is edited, Oktavia says how many it affects and, with the OK, updates their data and proposes a single network change in the Twin for all their PEs, respecting services that have a value of their own. Without the OK, the change applies only to new services.
  • A profile in use cannot be deleted. The table has a profile column and filter.

6. Service creation, change and retirement with Argos

Argos forms are filled from the Source of Truth, so engineers pick instead of typing:

  • From the SoT: pick the customer and the CE list is filtered by customer; choosing it fills its PE, the PE interface, the CE interface and the access VLAN reserved for that customer. Interfaces are drop-down lists, a warning appears if the VLAN is already used by another service on that port and, if the customer has two CEs, the pseudowire's B end takes the other one.
  • Your organization's data: each form carries the provider's custom fields for that service type (device-type fields as an SoT list). They are validated before touching the network: an invalid value neither saves the intent nor proposes the change. On save, they are stored in Customer services together with what Argos derives —VLAN, VRF, EVI, device—, and what was entered takes precedence.
  • Service profile: all three forms offer the profile, which fills the empty fields and puts its network parameters on the site or circuit (and enables the PE QoS if needed).
  • Dedicated internet PE + CE in one button: customer → PE and interface → CE and interface → VLAN → IP block. On save, the PE change and the CE change are proposed, linked in a service order: the CE is applied once the PE is confirmed and, after the last change is applied, Oktavia audits PE and CE, verifies the service and leaves the closure on the card: service up, or the failure. The VLAN and IP suggestion reuses what the customer has or had on that port, and the review warns if the creation would change the IP of something already configured.
  • A customer's QoS: the contract is applied to all its L3VPN sites in a single save and a single network change.
  • Retirement: from the table row or by asking Argos; it saves the intent without the service and proposes the change. Retiring dedicated internet is a single order, the CE first and then the PE.

The addressing plan for L3VPNs and pseudowires is documented in the forms and in Argos' context. Nothing is applied without approval.

Workflows

The same provisioning can also be built as workflows (Intent › Workflows). Customer pseudowire provisioning: pick the customer from a list; the workflow takes its next subnet and VLAN and both PE↔CE endpoints, saves the circuit, proposes the service order and waits for the PE and then the CE to be approved and confirmed; it then continues with the xconnect verification and the notification. L3VPN site provisioning: customer, site and CE from SoT lists, with the customer's numbering and the PE-CE eBGP verification. Also IP SLA on the CEs of a group and role hardening. The workflow map simulates everything with real data —nothing is written— and animates it step by step before running it from Argos.

7. The closed loop and the Digital Twin

  1. Audit the devices in scope over NETCONF (IOS XR) and SSH (CE).
  2. Compare each service's intended YANG configuration with the running one, leaf by leaf, and test the network state.
  3. Propose the change against the intent in the Digital Twin.
  4. Review: per device, which services are new, which change and which are removed compared with the last applied change, or "Nothing: same intent", so it can be cancelled.
  5. Approve. A person, always.
  6. Apply with prior validation and confirmed commit.
  7. Verify and audit again to close.

Core network lane

A core change is a transaction over all the IOS XR devices in the change at once:

  1. Validation on each IOS XR candidate configuration, as a dry run.
  2. Confirmed commit on all of them, with the window's countdown in view.
  3. Automatic verification: IS-IS adjacencies, core BGP sessions and xconnects up.
  4. Human confirmation of the change.

If one IOS XR rejects the commit or the verification fails, the change is cancelled on all of them. CEs go through their own Digital Twin change.

Change control

  • A change can be cancelled from its card at any stage before it is applied (draft, review, approved or dry run), and an administrator can clean up unfinished drafts.
  • What is saved in a scope travels in one change, with the devices of that save.
  • A change that went into some devices and failed on others is shown as partially applied: how many went in, which one failed and why; the retry builds a change with only what is missing.
  • The loop gives no verdict on a device it could not read: the step fails or closes "with pending items".
  • Confirming from Argos audits the devices in the change, so the loop closes with fresh data.

Per-service compliance

Each service has its own configuration result, and the state tests in the next section are added to it.

9. Visibility and diagnosis

Layered topology

  • Each device carries its layer —core, PE or CE—: from its SoT role or, without a provider role, from what it has configured. The site map marks each site's layer and the detail view each device's.
  • Services topology at scale: the core in the center, the points of presence on the sides and customers outward from their POP. With many customers, the POP carries an "N customer sites" card with their sessions and how many fail; hundreds of sites fit in the same drawing.
  • Core point-to-point links, the IS-IS layer and pseudowires; a connections table when a device or link is selected; hierarchical, star or grid layouts; diagram export as SVG.

Control plane and path

  • IS-IS neighbors with level, area and adjacency SID; BGP with its ASN; route table with VPN labels and TI-LFA backups told apart from ECMP; xconnect state.
  • Path across the MPLS L3VPN: ingress PE, VPN label and egress PE, in both directions, also on the Topology.

Diagnosis

  • Diagnose per service and Diagnose customer, from the customer page or by asking Argos.
  • Start from a ticket: Argos recognizes the sites in the customer's text, audits their configuration and state and summarizes the findings.
  • Demo scenario (INC-5101): on the test network, a customer CE stops advertising its site LAN over eBGP; the session stays up but the VRF loses the route. It is prepared and restored with Twin changes, never directly, to practice diagnosis and remediation with Argos.

Telemetry and technical report

  • CPU and memory, BGP sessions, IS-IS adjacencies and route table size over SNMP, and a pseudowire section with traffic and access circuit state on each PE, where the contracted bandwidth is 100 %. Over streaming, IOS XR also sends xconnect state.
  • Technical report with a customer services section: numbering plan, sites, circuits and QoS.

10. The NOC: customer impact and alerts

Which customers does it affect?

For any PE, P/RR or CE, Oktavia says which customer services go down, which are degraded (a dual-PE site) and which stay protected by TI-LFA, with the contract, the SLA and the monthly revenue at risk. Without picking a device, it delivers the ranking of single points of failure in the network, the one risking the most revenue first. No AI: explicit per-role rules. The customer page also shows the impact if its PE goes down.

Provider events and NOC email

The event orchestrator recognizes provider events —pseudowire down, IS-IS adjacency or BFD session down, VPNv4/EVPN session with the route reflector down— and the NOC email carries the customer impact: which services go down, of which customer, their SLA and the revenue at risk, with links to Customer services, Topology and Argos. Provider rules load in one click and each has its "test email" with real data.

11. New services and parameters, without development

A provider's engineer does not need to know NETCONF or write service packages to change what the device allows:

  • Change a service: one question ("what do you want to change?"), a form with range and default, an automatic preview of what changes on each device and a single button: Save and propose the change. Added parameters then show among the service's declared values.
  • NETCONF Dictionary: the parameters the device accepts, read from its own YANG models, searchable as in the CLI —"neighbor timers keepalive", "isis lsp-gen-interval", "interface mtu"—, by pasting an XR line or by telling Argos what is needed (the phrase is translated into the search, without AI). Picking one, Oktavia builds the extension: the XML, the field with its range and its removal, and it adds on its own the leaves the device requires together, such as a BGP neighbor's keepalive and holdtime.
  • Explain it with Argos: on a dictionary leaf's page, Argos (local AI or Claude) explains what it is, when it is used, its IOS XR CLI line, example values within its range and a caution. Only that leaf's data travels, and a fixed glossary anchors the acronyms.
  • Service Builder: pick options from the device's YANG, see the service as IOS XR CLI and as NETCONF XML, translate CLI ⇄ XML and publish it with Save and propose. Step by step in the services guide.
  • From XML: paste the XML you want on the device and turn it into a service parameter, without AI.
  • From a configuration, without AI: for CLI devices, such as the CE, paste the configuration and click each value that changes per device; each mark becomes a named parameter and the configuration becomes the service template. Without marks, the configuration goes in as is with its enable switch. Lines with secrets are not used and the count is reported.
  • With AI, if preferred: Argos (local AI or Claude) drafts the service from a description and an example configuration, and receives the marked parameters with their name and value: it does not guess them.
  • Retire here: a custom service declaration is removed by showing what each device receives to withdraw it, and a single change is proposed. A service created without coding is also retired without coding.

Everything passes through the gate before publishing (syntax, forbidden commands, clear-text secrets, impact on other services) and reaches the network through the Digital Twin, with prior validation and confirmed commit.

12. Automation with the API, Ansible and Python

Each customer service is orchestrated the same way from Argos or from an Ansible playbook: creating a pseudowire or an L3VPN site, a service's ODN color or a customer's multicast. Playbooks call the same APIs with per-tenant automation keys —shown only once, stored hashed and revocable—: they run the preview or save the intent and return the change proposed in the Twin. Approving and applying remain a person's job. In Argos, each flow has Do it with Ansible with the equivalent command. Step by step in the article Automate Oktavia with its API, Ansible and Python.

13. Contracts, usage and per-customer FinOps

Each customer has its Contract: a light CRM with what it bought. This is commercial data: the customer's configuration stays in the intent.

PartWhat it holds
RecordThe customer ID in the provider's CRM, its tier (gold, silver or bronze), notes and the customer custom fields.
Contracted servicesService or circuit ID, type (L3VPN, pseudowire or dedicated internet), profile, site, PE ↔ CE, contracted Mb, SLA, reference price, status and the service custom fields.

Administrators edit contracts and create new customers. Customers that exist only in the base, with no service in the network, are listed separately.

Real usage against the contract

For each service, Oktavia takes the last hour of traffic —average and peak— on the PE→CE interface, from the PE's SNMP telemetry, and compares it with the contracted Mb. The result is ok, near (80 % or more) or exceeds. Without telemetry or without an interface, it says so.

Per-customer FinOps

Under FinOps → Per customer, each device's monthly cost —amortized CAPEX, license, support and energy— is split among the services that use it, by bandwidth. With the contract's reference price, revenue, cost and margin per customer follow. It exists only in provider tenants: enterprise FinOps does not change.

Sites and devices

Edit site adds free attributes (key = value) and the customer the site belongs to. Each device has a Domain field —Core, Managed services…—, filled by role and editable, and its own free attributes.

15. Local AI with provider context

Local AI answers on the customer's server, sending nothing outside, with the context of a provider network:

  • The team's documents: design (HLD), runbooks, change procedure, security policy and circuits.
  • The per-site technical report.
  • The IOS XR guides from xrdocs: telemetry and Converged SDN Transport.
  • The technology RFCs: RFC 4364 (L3VPN), RFC 4456 (route reflector), RFC 7432 (EVPN), RFC 8214 (EVPN-VPWS), RFC 8402 (Segment Routing), RFC 8667 (IS-IS with Segment Routing), RFC 1195 (IS-IS), and the TI-LFA documentation.

It also explains NETCONF Dictionary leaves and drafts new services with no per-question cost. How it works is covered in the article Local AI with your company's context.

16. Deployment and security

  • One codebase, two modes. Oktavia SP is the same platform as Oktavia Pro; provider mode comes from the tenant's inventory and is not configured separately.
  • Installation. Containers on a Linux server with Docker, on the provider's premises or in its private cloud. Sizing by device count is in the Oktavia Pro technical sheet.
  • Connections in view.
