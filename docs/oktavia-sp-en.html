1. What it is

Oktavia SP is the Oktavia installation for service providers: the carrier service orchestrator that runs the core and edge of the network: the P routers and route reflectors in the core, the PEs at each point of presence, and the customer CEs. It is not a separate product: it is the same cycle of auditing, comparing against intent, proposing in the Digital Twin, approving, applying, verifying and auditing again, with a provider-specific service catalog.

It is tested in the lab with Cisco IOS XR (XRv9000 24.3) in the core and Cisco IOS on the customer CE.

RoleSite typeDevices
P and route reflectorcoreIOS XR core
PEpopIOS XR provider edge
CEcliente (customer)Customer device, Cisco IOS

2. The approach

Intent per service, not per device

Intent is declared per service, in the style of L3SM and L2SM: a customer service is declared once —the customer, its sites or circuits and the contracted bandwidth— and Oktavia distributes it to every PE and CE involved. Nobody edits device by device.

Each customer's numbering plan is derived from its identifier: the customer AS, the RTs, the RD per PE, the /31 PE-CE links, the pseudowire EVI and the subnets. Two customers never collide, and the same customer is numbered the same way on every rollout.

Model-driven on IOS XR

IOS XR devices are managed over NETCONF with YANG models: the Cisco-IOS-XR-um-* models for configuration and the native operational models for state. No screen scraping.

Closed loop with a network transaction

The core is not changed device by device: every IOS XR enters a single transaction. If one rejects the change or verification fails, it is cancelled on all of them.

The provider tenant is recognized automatically

When an organization has IOS XR devices or P, PE or RR roles, Oktavia treats it as a provider network: the domains become Transport · Services · Security · Management (in an enterprise they are Campus · WAN · Security · Management) in Argos, Architecture, Telemetry, Home and the analyses, and the campus flows —VLANs, port-security, FlexVPN and the campus test network— are hidden. Other tenants are not affected.

The customer side comes from the same service. The CE (Cisco IOS) part of the L3VPN and the pseudowires is generated from the same declaration as the PE part: there is no second intent to keep up to date.

Viewing and testing an IOS XR service

On IOS XR there is no Jinja template: the NETCONF lane builds the YANG XML from the same values. On the service page, testing with a device shows its XML, with the device's values highlighted to tell them apart from what is fixed in the lane. See what changes and Test these values compare today's NETCONF plan with the plan for your change, as the Digital Twin would push it, with commit confirmed. This applies to core and customer services. Step by step in the Services and templates guide.

4. The cycle and the Digital Twin

Core network lane

A core change is one transaction over every IOS XR at once:

  1. Validation in each IOS XR candidate configuration, as a dry run.
  2. Commit confirmed on all of them.
  3. Automatic verification: IS-IS adjacencies, core BGP sessions and xconnects up.
  4. Human confirmation of the change.

If an IOS XR rejects the commit or verification fails, the change is cancelled on all of them. CEs go through their own Digital Twin change, device by device.

Compliance per service

The desired YANG configuration of each service is compared with the running configuration, leaf by leaf, and each service gets its own result. State tests are added on top: IS-IS adjacencies, BFD and the provider BGP sessions.

5. Visibility

  • Desired configuration of each IOS XR, per service.
  • Topology with the core point-to-point links, the IS-IS layer and the pseudowires.
  • Control plane: IS-IS neighbors with level, area and adjacency SID; BGP with its ASN; routing table with VPN labels and TI-LFA backups told apart from ECMP.
  • Path across the MPLS L3VPN: ingress PE, VPN label and egress PE, in both directions, also on the Topology.
  • Customer services: one page per customer with its L3VPNs and pseudowires, end-to-end state, QoS contract, diagram and its commercial contract (section 6).
  • Telemetry: CPU and memory, BGP sessions, IS-IS adjacencies and routing table size over SNMP, and a pseudowires section with the traffic and attachment-circuit state on each PE, where the contracted bandwidth is 100 %. Over streaming, IOS XR also sends xconnect state.
  • Technical record with a customer services section: numbering plan, sites, circuits and QoS.

Beyond configuration, Oktavia tests that the network works as declared, reading IOS XR operational models over NETCONF:

MulticastThe CE of every multicast site is a PIM neighbor of the PE in the customer VRF.

In Argos every service has its “State of…” button —the core, L3VPNs, pseudowires, Dedicated Internet and QoS— with configuration ✓/✗ per device, its network state and telemetry when available. The Transport console shows Core health, and the Architecture view and the technical report include a Provider services section: each service's declared design, its numbering, which customers use it and how it is doing today.

7. The NOC: customer impact and alerts

Which customers are affected?

For any PE, P/RR or CE, Oktavia tells which customer services go down, which are degraded (a dual-homed site) and which stay protected by TI-LFA, with the contract, the SLA and the monthly revenue at risk from the CRM. Without choosing a device, it returns the network's single points of failure, highest revenue at risk first. No AI: explicit rules per role.

Provider events and NOC email

The event orchestrator understands provider events —pseudowire down, IS-IS adjacency or BFD session down, VPNv4/EVPN session with the route reflector down— and the NOC email carries the customer impact: which services go down, for which customer, their SLA and revenue at risk, with links to Customer services, Topology and Argos. Provider rules load with one click and each one has a “test email” with real data.

8. New services and parameters, without development

A provider engineer does not need to know NETCONF to change what the device allows:

  • Change a service: one question (“what do you want to change?”), a form with range and default, an automatic preview of what changes on each device and a single button: Save and propose the change.
  • NETCONF dictionary: the parameters the device accepts, read from its own YANG models, searchable like the CLI —“neighbor timers keepalive”, “isis lsp-gen-interval”, “interface mtu”— or by pasting an XR line. Pick one and Oktavia builds the extension: the XML, the field with its range and its removal.
  • From XML: paste the XML you want on the device and turn it into a service parameter, no AI.
  • A catalog of proven extensions and the tenant's custom services.

Everything goes through the gate before publishing (syntax, forbidden commands, plaintext secrets, impact on other services) and reaches the network through the Digital Twin, with prior validation and confirmed commit.

9. Automation with the API, Ansible and Python

Every customer service is orchestrated the same way from Argos or from an Ansible playbook: adding a pseudowire, an L3VPN site, a service's ODN color or a customer's multicast. Playbooks call the same APIs with per-tenant automation keys —shown once, stored hashed and revocable—: they preview or save the intent and return the change proposed in the Twin. Approving and applying is still a person's call. In Argos every flow has Do it with Ansible with the equivalent command. Step by step in the article Automate Oktavia with its API, Ansible and Python.

10. Customers and contracts

In Customer services, each customer has its Contract: a lightweight CRM with what it bought. This is commercial data: the customer's configuration still lives in the intent.

PartWhat it holds
ProfileThe customer ID in your CRM, its tier (gold, silver or bronze) and notes.
Contracted servicesService or circuit ID, type (L3VPN, pseudowire or dedicated internet), customer-type site, PE ↔ CE, contracted Mb, SLA and reference price.

Administrators edit contracts and add new customers. Customers that exist only in the CRM, with no service on the network, are listed separately.

Actual usage against the contract

For each service, Oktavia takes the last hour of traffic —average and peak— on the PE→CE interface, from the PE's SNMP telemetry, and compares it with the contracted Mb. The result is ok, near (80 % or more) or exceeds.

FinOps per customer

In FinOps → Per customer, the monthly cost of each device —amortized CAPEX, license, support and power— is split among the services that use it, by their bandwidth. With the contract's reference price you get revenue, cost and margin per customer. It exists only on provider tenants: an enterprise's FinOps does not change.

Sites and devices

Edit site adds free attributes (key = value) and, at a provider, the customer the site belongs to. Each device has a Domain field —Transport, Services…—, filled in by role and editable, and its own free attributes.

11. Argos on a provider network

Argos is your SP architect: the Custom orchestration tab gathers the flows built for the provider's architecture and standards. On a provider tenant it offers:

FlowWhat it does
SP core (IOS XR)Applies the core intent as a network change.
Customer pseudowires (EVPN-VPWS)Shows the circuits with their state and adds a pseudowire: validates VLAN, subnet and endpoints, saves the intent and proposes the network change.
Customer QoS (contracted bandwidth)Shows and changes contracts: validates, saves and proposes the change.
Customer L3VPNAdd and remove a customer or site, L3VPN state and customer multicast.
Customer Dedicated InternetAdd, change and remove on the PE and its CE side.
Traffic engineering (SR-TE ODN)Policy state, colors per service, assign and remove a color on an L3VPN or on a customer's pseudowires.
Change a serviceAdd or move a parameter of any service with a preview and a single button.

The common tabs take on a provider meaning:

  • Diagnosis and analysis per domain, Transport and Services.
  • Connectivity test of a customer site from the CE: reachability to the PE, the PE-CE BGP session, the remote LAN across the L3VPN and the remote CE of the pseudowire.
  • Enable telemetry with IOS XR streaming.

On a provider tenant, Argos shows only what applies to a provider. In the Summary, the Customers and contracts group brings together Customer services, FinOps per customer and Customer QoS.

As everywhere else in the platform, no action that touches the network happens without a person approving it.

12. Local AI with provider context

The local AI answers, on the customer's own server, with the context of a provider network:

  • The team's documents: design (HLD), runbooks, change procedure, security policy and circuits.
  • The technical record per site.
  • The IOS XR guides from xrdocs: telemetry and Converged SDN Transport.
  • The RFCs of the technologies: RFC 4364 (L3VPN), RFC 4456 (route reflector), RFC 7432 (EVPN), RFC 8214 (EVPN-VPWS), RFC 8402 (Segment Routing), RFC 8667 (IS-IS with Segment Routing), RFC 1195 (IS-IS), and the TI-LFA documentation.

How it works is covered in the article Local AI with your company's context.
